---
title: Cantonese steamed fish
description: A white fish fillet steamed until just flaky, finished with sizzling ginger, scallion and a light soy sauce.
date: '2026-07-16'
totalMinutes: 20
yield: 2 servings
ingredientTags: [Fish, Spring onions]
occasions: [Weeknight dinners, Cooking for two]
source:
  name: The Woks of Life
  url: https://thewoksoflife.com/cantonese-steamed-fish/
ingredients:
  - name: Fish
    items:
      - 10 oz fillet of delicate white fish, such as sea bass, sole, flounder, tilapia or haddock
  - name: Aromatics
    items:
      - 2 scallions, cut into 2-inch lengths and julienned – whites kept separate
      - 2 tbsp ginger (about 15 g), julienned
      - 1 small bunch cilantro, roughly chopped
  - name: Sauce
    items:
      - 1½ tbsp light soy sauce
      - ⅛ tsp salt
      - ⅛ tsp sugar
      - 2 tbsp hot water
  - name: Oil
    items:
      - 2 tbsp vegetable oil
steps:
  - Stir the salt and sugar into the soy sauce and hot water until dissolved.
  - Bring 1–2 inches of water to a boil in a steamer.
  - Rinse the fish, set it on a heatproof plate in the steamer, and turn the heat to medium.
  - Cover and steam 7–10 minutes (4–5 for very thin fillets), until a butter knife slides easily through the thickest part.
  - Turn off the heat, pour off the liquid on the plate, and scatter a third of the aromatics over the fish.
  - Heat the oil in a wok over medium-high. Fry the remaining ginger 1 minute, then the scallion whites 30 seconds.
  - Add the rest of the scallions and cilantro, then the sauce. Let it bubble about 30 seconds, until wilted.
  - Pour the sauce over the fish and serve at once.
notes:
  - For more sauce, double the aromatics and sauce for the same fillet.
  - Avoid oily, firm fish such as mackerel or swordfish.
  - For extra sizzle, heat another tablespoon of oil and pour it over the raw aromatics at the end.
---
A quick Cantonese classic. The fish is steamed plain, then dressed with hot oil, ginger, scallion and seasoned soy.
