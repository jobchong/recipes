---
title: Steamed garlic shrimp on vermicelli (蒜蓉蒸蝦)
description: Head-on shrimp stuffed with garlic paste and steamed over seasoned mung bean noodles.
date: '2026-08-11'
totalMinutes: 35
yield: 4 servings
ingredientTags: [Prawns, Noodles]
occasions: [Something to share]
source:
  name: Made With Lau
  url: https://www.madewithlau.com/recipes/steamed-garlic-shrimp
ingredients:
  - name: Vermicelli
    items:
      - 3 oz mung bean vermicelli
      - 1 tbsp oyster sauce
      - ½ tsp light soy sauce
      - ½ tsp salt
      - ½ tsp sugar
  - name: Garlic paste
    items:
      - 2 oz garlic (6–7 cloves), minced to a paste
      - ½ oz green onion, finely sliced – whites in the paste, greens to garnish
      - ½ oz ginger, minced to a paste
  - name: Hot oil
    items:
      - 1½ tbsp oil
  - name: Paste seasoning
    items:
      - ½ tsp salt
      - 1 tsp sugar
      - ¼ tsp white pepper
      - 1 tsp oyster sauce
      - 1 tsp light soy sauce
      - ½ tsp sesame oil
      - 1 tsp cornstarch
  - name: Shrimp
    items:
      - 12 oz head-on shrimp (31/40)
steps:
  - Soak the vermicelli in warm water 10 minutes. Snip it shorter, drain, and squeeze dry.
  - Toss it with its seasonings until evenly brown, then spread it on a heatproof plate.
  - Put the garlic, green onion whites, and ginger in a bowl.
  - Heat the oil on high until it just smokes, 30–40 seconds, and pour it over the bowl.
  - Stir in the paste seasoning, adding the cornstarch last.
  - Devein each shrimp with a toothpick just behind the head, trim the head and legs, and slit the shell to make a pocket.
  - Fill each shrimp with ½–1 tsp paste. Too much overpowers the shrimp.
  - Arrange the shrimp in a ring on the noodles and spoon any leftover paste into the gaps.
  - Bring the steamer water to a rolling boil and steam on high 6–7 minutes, until the shells turn red and the meat is opaque.
  - Garnish with the green onion greens.
notes:
  - Head-on shrimp are best, since their juices flavour the noodles.
  - Frozen shrimp are fine. Thaw them overnight in the fridge, or in their bag in cold water.
---
A Cantonese banquet dish. As the shrimp steam, their juices run down into the glass noodles underneath.
