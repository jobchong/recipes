---
title: Shanghai braised pork chops (葱烤排骨)
description: Pounded, lightly fried pork chops braised over a bed of scallions in a sweet soy glaze.
date: '2026-10-08'
totalMinutes: 60
yield: 6 servings
ingredientTags: [Pork, Spring onions]
occasions: [Something to share, Slow weekends]
source:
  name: The Woks of Life
  url: https://thewoksoflife.com/braised-pork-chops-shanghai/
ingredients:
  - name: Pork
    items:
      - 2 lb boneless center-cut pork chops (6 pieces)
  - name: Marinade
    items:
      - 2 tbsp Shaoxing wine
      - ½ tsp salt
      - 1 tbsp light soy sauce
      - 3–4 tbsp water
  - name: Coating
    items:
      - 2 medium eggs
      - ⅓ cup cornstarch
  - name: Oil
    items:
      - ⅓ cup neutral oil
  - name: Scallions
    items:
      - 2–3 bunches scallions, halved crosswise, thick stems split
  - name: Braise
    items:
      - 15 g rock sugar (or 1 tbsp sugar)
      - 1¼–1½ cups water
      - 2 tbsp light soy sauce
      - 1½ tbsp dark soy sauce
steps:
  - Snip the fat along the edge of each chop twice so it won't curl. Pound to ¼–½ inch thick.
  - Toss the chops with the marinade until it's absorbed.
  - Mix in the eggs, then the cornstarch. Rest 15 minutes.
  - Wash the scallions, dry them well, and cut them.
  - Heat the wok on medium-high until smoking and add the oil. Fry the chops in two batches, 30–60 seconds a side, until light golden. Any longer and they toughen.
  - Leave about 2 tbsp oil in the wok. On medium, cook the scallions until wilted and browned at the edges.
  - Lay the chops on the scallions, add the braise, cover, and simmer 3 minutes.
  - Flip the chops into the sauce, cover, and simmer 2–3 minutes more.
  - Move the chops to a platter. On high, reduce the sauce 1–2 minutes until glossy, leaving some loose for the rice.
  - Top the chops with the scallions and sauce, and serve with rice.
notes:
  - Bone-in chops need about a minute longer at each simmer.
  - Pork loin cut into ½-inch slices works the same way.
  - Use less water on a weak stove and more on a very hot one.
---
A Shanghai home-style dish. A generous amount of scallions melts into the sweet soy sauce under the chops.
